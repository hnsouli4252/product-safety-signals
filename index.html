Method & evidence boundaries

What qualifies, what is excluded, and how timing is calculated.

Independent harm reporting before action

The recall set combines CPSC’s official downloadable recall file through August 20 with the agency’s current recall index for the ten August 27 announcements. Product, brand, model, hazard mechanics, harm, publication date, and agency-action date were reviewed before a match was promoted.

Qualifying rule

  • Actual harm: the article reports a consumer injury or death involving a product; a theoretical hazard alone does not qualify.
  • Independent reporting: the article’s substance is consumer-harm reporting, not a rewrite or announcement of a CPSC or firm recall/alert.
  • Strictly pre-action: publication must be earlier than the relevant Recall or Safety Alert date. Same-day and post-action coverage is excluded from early-warning timing.
  • Defensible match: product identity or product-family scope and the injury mechanism must align. Category-only matches are labeled, scored lower, and excluded from exact/probable-product recall averages.

Calculations

The Early warnings view calculates elapsed days to the labeled agency action for both Recalls and Safety Alerts. Its three reviewable signals are sorted 497, 206, and 58 days. The narrower dashboard average and longest pre-recall metrics use exact/probable-product Recall matches only; both remain 206 days from the Brookstone case. The Cuisinart signal is not included in that average because CBS identified a metal-wire grill brush, but not its brand or model.

Past-year U.S. feed rule

The full feed retains 25 independent articles across 15 categories about actual U.S. consumer-product injuries or deaths published during the past year. It excludes stories whose substance is a recall or agency announcement. Every lead is labeled Pre-action, Post-action, or No linked action; these labels describe sequence only. Sixteen no-linked-action leads receive an explicit bounded-search note and a suggested analyst follow-up area. International incidents remain outside the primary feed, and seven recall-announcement articles remain excluded audit records.

Recent focused subset

Four no-linked-action leads published August 17–30, 2026 are repeated in a prominent focused view. The exact label “No linked CPSC action located in review” means only that no matching product-specific Recall or Safety Alert was found in the bounded review through August 30, 2026. It is not proof of absence and is not a recommendation that CPSC act.

Sequence does not establish that an incident or article caused, accelerated, or influenced agency action. Lawsuit reporting describes allegations, not adjudicated findings. Suggested follow-up areas are analyst prompts, not defect findings or regulatory recommendations.

Human review checklist

  • Verify the article date falls within August 31, 2025–August 30, 2026 and the incident is U.S.-reported.
  • Confirm the article reports an injury or death—not only a hazard or recall.
  • Verify product/model identifiers, hazard mechanics, and action-search results.
  • Keep category-level signals visibly separate from exact-product matches.
  • Record the analyst’s decision before operational use.

News Evidence Methodology

How the search system finds, rejects, ranks, clusters, and audits candidate harm reporting.

Multi-stage search workflow

The system searches for independent public reporting of actual consumer injuries or deaths associated with recalled products.

For each recall, the system expands product names, model identifiers, manufacturers, hazards, injury mechanisms, locations, and related terminology into multiple search queries. It searches progressively backward from the recall announcement date and reviews a broad set of candidate articles.

Candidate evaluation

  • Product identity, with extra weight for exact models and identifiers
  • Evidence of an actual injury or death
  • Hazard and event similarity
  • Publication timing and location
  • Source quality, including credible local outlets and authorities
  • Whether the article is independent reporting or primarily recall-announcement coverage

Statements that a product “can cause injury or death” do not qualify as harm evidence unless the article describes an actual incident. Recall-announcement coverage is excluded from qualification. Articles published after the recall may be retained as contextual evidence but do not qualify as pre-recall signals.

Ranking and clustering

Candidate scores weight product identity 30%, actual harm 25%, hazard similarity 15%, timing 15%, source quality 10%, and geography 5%, with penalties for recall coverage, post-recall timing, weak product identity, generic hazard language, and duplicates.

Multiple articles describing the same event are grouped into one incident. The earliest qualifying independent article in the incident cluster is used when calculating pre-recall lead time.

Search results are algorithmically ranked but remain subject to human source review. The system is intended for decision support and signal discovery, not adjudication.

Reproducibility & performance

Pipeline

  1. Recall ingestion
  2. Entity normalization
  3. Query generation
  4. Candidate retrieval
  5. Classification and ranking
  6. Incident clustering

Progressive windows

0–30 days · 31–90 days · 91–365 days · 1–3 years · 3–10 years. A normal run stops after a high-confidence qualified candidate; exhaustive mode searches every window.

Caching

Query results, fetched article content, and classifications use separate caches. Classification entries include article content, methodology, and classifier versions, so model changes cannot silently reuse stale decisions.

Audit record

Every run records versions, queries, windows, URLs retrieved, candidates evaluated, incident clusters, qualified counts, selected candidate, and timestamps.

Human overrides

Reviewer states—unreviewed, confirmed, rejected, and needs more research—are stored separately from model-generated classification. A new model run does not overwrite them.

Evaluation gates

Known-harm recall ≥95% · top-five precision ≥90% · recall-announcement false qualification 0% · duplicate incident rate <5%. Cached p95 target <4 seconds; fresh initial p95 target <12 seconds.
