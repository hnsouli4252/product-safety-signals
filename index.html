Method & evidence boundaries

What the dashboard can support—and what it cannot.

Conservative matching by design

The recall set combines CPSC’s official downloadable recall file (which contained announcements through August 20 when retrieved) with the agency’s current recall index for the ten August 27 announcements. Product names, brands, models, hazard mechanics, incident descriptions, dates, and recall scale were compared before a news item was linked.

Timeline rules

  • Recall announcement: the date shown by CPSC, linked to the agency notice.
  • Consumer incident: the earliest public SaferProducts.gov/CPSC report date only when a dated public report can be verified. A CPSC notice’s undated incident count is preserved, but is not converted into a date.
  • News: the earliest qualifying public article located in the bounded search. Exact product/brand and injury facts receive high confidence; ambiguous candidates are not promoted.

Important limits

The SaferProducts public service returned an HTTP 503 during this review. The dashboard therefore does not claim an earliest incident-report date unless one was independently visible in official public material. “Not located” means only that a match was not found in this review; it is not evidence of absence.

No element of a timeline implies that an incident or news article caused, accelerated, or influenced a recall. Search coverage is not global or exhaustive. All records remain decision-support leads requiring human verification.

Human review checklist

  • Open the CPSC notice and verify affected model identifiers.
  • Open the article and confirm its date, named product, harm, and source attribution.
  • Distinguish a hazard statement from an incident that actually occurred.
  • Do not merge similar hazards across brands without product-level evidence.
  • Record the analyst’s decision outside this static prototype before operational use.

Matching confidence

95–100% is reserved for exact or near-exact product/brand matches with aligned incident facts. No confidence is shown when no qualifying article was located. Scores express match strength only—not truth, severity, or causation.
