Confidence is a triage aid—not a statement that an event occurred exactly as reported.

Methodology & limitations

How SignalDesk turns news coverage into reviewable product-safety signals.

Transparent by design

The prototype models a monitoring workflow across ten major outlets. It identifies candidate stories, extracts product and harm attributes, groups likely duplicates, assigns a confidence score, and routes ambiguous or consequential signals to a person.

Important: A “recall-risk signal” is an internal prioritization label. It is not an official recall determination, regulatory finding, or proof of product defect.

Matching and extraction

Candidate matching combines product-category language with explicit harm terms such as injury, death, burn, fall, poisoning, fire, or hospitalization. The extraction step captures product, brand, incident type, date, location when available, quoted or paraphrased evidence, and source provenance.

Deduplication

Likely copies and follow-up stories are clustered using normalized product/brand names, event date and location, harm type, and semantic similarity. Analysts can split incorrectly merged clusters or merge remaining duplicates.

Confidence scoring

  • Source evidence (35%): specificity and directness of the report.
  • Entity certainty (25%): clarity of product and brand identification.
  • Harm certainty (25%): explicit injury or fatality linkage.
  • Corroboration (15%): independent reporting or primary evidence.

Thresholds in the demo: 85–100 high confidence, 70–84 moderate, below 70 mandatory review. Confidence does not measure legal causation.

Limitations

News coverage is incomplete and uneven. Reports may omit model numbers, misidentify products, repeat early claims, or evolve as investigations continue. Some incidents receive no media attention. Automated extraction can make mistakes, and outlet access, paywalls, syndication, and API terms affect coverage. Records must be checked against source material and authoritative regulator notices before action.
